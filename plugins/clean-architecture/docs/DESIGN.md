# Clean Architecture plugin: component map

This file is the plan the components were built from. It lists every component with its purpose, the upstream sections it applies, and the one-sentence reason it is not a duplicate of its nearest sibling.
Upstream sections are the `##` headings (and the `###` headings under them) of `skills/principles/references/full.md`.

## Design decisions

1. **The upstream rule set is short (515 lines, 18 sections), so components are separated by role and by situation, not by rewording.** Each rule cluster has a rules skill. Each cluster that has a reviewable symptom has one read-only reviewer with its own report shape. Each repeatable move (extract a use case, introduce a port, build a feature, enforce a boundary) has one manual workflow and, where the work is big enough to delegate, one implementer.
2. **Clusters follow the seams a reader meets in code**, not the order of the upstream bullets. The seven `Forbidden Patterns` families are distributed to the skill that owns the fix (framework leakage to `framework-independence`, database leakage to `persistence-independence` and `boundary-models`, controller-centric logic and layer bypass to `interface-adapters`, god services to `service-sprawl`, direction violations to `dependency-rule` and `ports-and-gateways`, utility dumping grounds to `shared-code-coupling`).
3. **Import direction is the plugin's central check.** The `dependency-rule` skill owns the rule, `import-direction-reviewer` judges it, `import-scanner` produces the raw edge census, `/clean-architecture:layer-map` discovers the layers a repository actually has, and `architecture-test-writer` plus `/clean-architecture:enforce-boundaries` make the rule executable.
4. **The book's own brake is built in.** The rule set says boundaries are chosen by volatility, policy importance, substitution value, testability and cost, and that a boundary whose cost exceeds the option value it preserves must not be built. `boundary-economics`, `boundary-cost-reviewer`, the `boundary-budget` style and the three implementers that create structure (`use-case-extractor`, `port-introducer`, `feature-builder`) carry that brake, so the toolkit does not turn a small CRUD change into five layers.
5. **Component-level principles come from one source line each, so they are labelled.** The upstream text states three component rules (group by cohesion and release pressure, avoid cycles, balance stability and abstraction). `component-grouping` and `component-coupling` expand them with the book's well-known component principles and metrics. Everything beyond those three lines is marked "Added judgment" (the book's component principles, not in the vendored rule set).
6. **Model-invocable skills are capped at 20.** There are exactly 20. Everything that is a procedure with arguments is a manual skill (12).
7. **Reviewers own one concern and have their own output shape.** The import reviewer reports an edge ledger, the use-case reviewer reports use-case cards, the model reviewer reports a crossing table, the wiring reviewer reports construction sites, the component reviewer reports a component graph with instability numbers, and so on.
8. **Persistence has two reviewers because it has two symptoms.** ORM and driver imports, mapping markers and database-bound entities in inner layers are read by `import-direction-reviewer`; business decisions hidden in gateway code, queries and mappers are read by `adapter-humility-reviewer`. The hub and `/clean-architecture:review` route accordingly.
9. **Modal strength is tagged on every rule** as `MUST`, `SHOULD`, `MUST NOT` or `Added judgment`, following the source's own convention (`MUST` binding, `SHOULD` strong default, `MUST NOT` forbidden; "Prefer" is `SHOULD`; "Do not", "Avoid" and "Never" are `MUST NOT` unless the source softens them with "where possible" or "when avoidable"). Rules found only in `mini.md` are marked "compact rules".

## Provenance policy

- A rule with no label comes from `full.md`.
- "Compact rules" marks wording that exists only in `mini.md` or `nano.md` (for example "a package name or a shared `common` folder is not enough" and the "sideways coupling" phrase).
- "Added judgment" marks a heuristic of this plugin, a numeric threshold, or a concept from the book that the vendored rule set does not state (the instability metric, abstractness, the three component-cohesion principles by name, the acyclic and stable-dependency rules by name, and how to resolve the tension between "introduce a boundary sooner" and "do not overbuild boundaries").
- Examples are original. The domains used (subscription renewals, library loans, parking permits, shipping quotes, appointment booking, loyalty points, invoice reminders) are chosen to differ from the sibling plugins.

## Confusable pairs (model-invocable skills)

The descriptions of both skills in each pair carry a discriminating clause (checked against the final descriptions). Rule of thumb used when writing them: the symptom the user can see decides the skill.

| --- | --- | --- |

## Agent pairs that are close, and how they differ

- `import-direction-reviewer` and `import-scanner`: judgments versus numbers. The scanner never says "violation"; the reviewer never counts for its own sake.
- `import-direction-reviewer` and `component-coupling-reviewer`: the first walks imports against a layer map; the second walks components, cycles and instability.
- `adapter-humility-reviewer` and `service-edge-reviewer`: controllers, presenters, gateways and jobs versus message listeners, remote clients and device adapters.
- `port-ownership-reviewer` and `interface-contract-reviewer`: who defines and owns the interface versus whether the interface and its implementations are well formed.
- `structure-reviewer` and `sprawl-reviewer`: what the tree says versus what piles up inside a module.
- `use-case-extractor` and `/clean-architecture:extract-use-case`: the skill is the in-session loop with the user deciding names and field lists; the agent is delegated for several actions with a findings list and works without questions once the scope is clear. They follow the same procedure and differ in autonomy and report.
- `port-introducer` and `/clean-architecture:introduce-port`, `feature-builder` and `/clean-architecture:new-feature`, `architecture-test-writer` and `/clean-architecture:enforce-boundaries`: the same autonomy split.
- `import-scanner` and `/clean-architecture:layer-map`: the scanner is a delegable data-gatherer with no layer judgment (reviewers and the lead call it); `layer-map` is a user-run forked report that infers the layers from behavior and classifies each edge as inward or outward.
- `seam-test-writer` and `architecture-test-writer`: behavior tests with fakes versus structural tests on imports.
- `seam-test-writer` and `test-seam-reviewer`: the writer adds tests and never edits production code; the reviewer reports where tests sit and names the reviewer or implementer that owns the cause when policy cannot be tested without a detail.
- `place-code`, `adapter-humility-reviewer` and `use-case-extractor`: all three classify code statement by statement, for different jobs. `/clean-architecture:place-code` is the read-only per-snippet classifier the user runs on one unit; it says where each block belongs and edits nothing. `adapter-humility-reviewer` applies the same split to the adapters in a diff (translation, call, decision) and reports weight, meaning how much decision code each adapter holds. `use-case-extractor` classifies in order to move code and edits it. The three share the class names translation, orchestration, policy and infrastructure (the reviewer's `call` is the adapter's share of orchestration and its `decision` is policy), so a block classified by one can be handed to another without re-translating it.
- `domain-purity-reviewer` and `adapter-humility-reviewer`: the first starts from entities and asks what they permit; the second starts from adapters and asks what they decide. A rule found in outer code while checking an invariant is handed over by name, not reported twice.
- `use-case-extractor` and `port-introducer`: one edits at the action (a handler becomes a use case, creating ports as needed), the other at the dependency (a direct call becomes a port). Neither renames public names or changes behavior.

## Overlap review

Pairs that were close and how they were separated:

- The three leak skills (`dependency-rule`, `framework-independence`, `persistence-independence`) and the model skill `boundary-models` all start from "core code knows about a detail". They differ in the evidence a reader sees: an import edge, a framework marker, a table or SQL shape, a type in a signature.
- `use-cases`, `entities-and-invariants` and `interface-adapters` are the three places a business rule can sit. The rule's home is the first; the two others are where it must not be, in opposite directions.
- `boundary-economics` is the only skill that may recommend not adding structure. Every other skill in the plugin assumes the boundary has been justified, and says so in its trade-offs section.
- `testing-seams` does not judge test style. It judges whether the policy can be tested at all without a framework, database or network, and where each kind of test belongs.

Upstream sections that have no dedicated skill are covered as follows: `Purpose` and `Final Instruction` are the hub's frame and the tie-breakers in `boundary-economics`; `Review Checklist` is the content of `checklist`, `final-gate` and the hub's final checklist; `Output Expectations` is the reporting contract of `review`, `new-feature`, `fix` and the styles; `Preferred Default Shapes` is the target of `new-feature` and the examples of the cluster skills.
